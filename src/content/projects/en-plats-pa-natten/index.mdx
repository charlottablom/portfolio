## Context

"A place at night" was the guiding phrase behind this high-rise on Första Långgatan in Gothenburg, close to the new Masthuggskajen and the rich nightlife of Andra Långgatan and Stigbergstorget. Built entirely in timber, it houses a mix of uses - delicatessen, restaurant, office, co-working, and a rooftop bar with conference space.

## Methods

The ground floor is organized as a market hall, its stalls arranged like a street network around a central square - open for temporary market vendors by day, and for the neighbourhood's dance floor by night.

## Process

Above, the first floor holds four restaurants, each with its own kitchen, dish-washing area, and till, arranged around the large opening in the floor slab down to the market hall. That opening keeps the restaurants visible from the ground floor and connects them to both the public market below and the office floors above.

<figure id="img-restaurantfloor">

By night, that same double-height opening turns the market hall into a stage: a bridge across the void becomes somewhere to look down from, while the floor below fills with dancing.

## Outcomes

Framed as "the building as a system," the high-rise was designed rationally around its function and technical systems, to make future spatial reorganization easy. Its load-bearing structure of glulam columns follows a grid spaced to functional room dimensions, so the building could one day be transformed into, for example, housing.

<figure id="img-loads">
  <LoadingImage src={loadsDiagram} width={1600} alt="Axonometric diagram of the building's structural frame, color-coded for vertical load, horizontal load, and supported elements, with glulam columns spaced on a regular grid." />

</figure>

That same rationality carries down to a single construction detail, resolving the facade and floor slab's build-up - insulation, vapor barrier, and cladding - against an office fitted out above.

<figure id="img-facadedetail">
  <LoadingImage src={facadeDetail} width={1600} alt="A detailed construction section through the facade and floor slab at 1:20, showing the wall build-up of insulation, vapor barrier, and cladding layers, beside an office scene with desks and chairs." />
  <figcaption>Facade and floor slab, detailed at 1:20.</figcaption>
</figure>

The stairwells are their own fire cells in concrete, placed against the facade - which keeps the upper floors' offices and co-working spaces running independently of the market hall's own opening hours. A separate section maps the building's technical systems - shafts, telecom, plumbing, and supply and exhaust air - running the height of the building alongside the stairs.
