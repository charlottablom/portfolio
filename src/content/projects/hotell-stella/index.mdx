## Context

Majnabbehamnen, a former ferry terminal on the Göta River, is being redeveloped into Knallen, a new waterfront district combining housing and other uses. Hotell Stella occupies the district's quay edge, its ground-floor restaurant close to the Klippan ferry crossing on the opposite bank.

## Methods

The district's street network is deliberately irregular: streets and building volumes meet at varying angles, creating sheltered nooks for outdoor life despite strong winds off the sea. The same geometry frames views toward landmarks that orient the district within the wider city - the Eriksberg crane and the Älvsborg Bridge.

Overlaid at district scale, that varied geometry produces a roof landscape of pockets and setbacks, offering space for outdoor seating and small public parks across the district.

## Outcomes

At ground level, the hotel is organized around a public front and a working back of house that must stay close to each other without crossing: the restaurant, lobby, and conference rooms face the street and quay, while the kitchen, laundry, and staff areas sit behind.

<figure id="img-groundfloor">
  <LoadingImage src={groundFloor} width={1600} alt="Ground floor plan of an elongated, wedge-shaped building showing a restaurant, bar, conference rooms, lobby, and administration at the front, with kitchen, laundry, and staff areas behind." />
  <figcaption>Ground floor plan: restaurant, lobby, and back of house.</figcaption>
</figure>

Guest rooms wrap around the atrium on the floors above, with suites placed at the building's prow for views toward the Älvsborg Bridge, and a rest room for housekeeping staff at the atrium's widest point.

The restaurant's terrace steps back from the street to form a wind-sheltered outdoor room looking out over the bridge.
